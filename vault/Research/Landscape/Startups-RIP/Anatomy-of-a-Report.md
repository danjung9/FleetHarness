# Anatomy of a public report

## Bottom line

[E] Four inspected reports share a recognizable historical outline and a separate proposal for what someone might build now. [E] Their strongest passages distinguish what a source establishes from what remains unknown. [R] Borrow the outline and the visible uncertainty. [R] Do not borrow a compulsory failure story or let a proposed minimum product masquerade as tested software.

## Inspection scope

[E] Public text and section navigation were inspected on 2026-11-04 at https://startups.rip/company/preflight, https://startups.rip/company/fig, https://startups.rip/company/humanloop and https://startups.rip/company/hamming-ai. [E] This was document inspection, not a test of authenticated features or a complete independent investigation of the companies. [E] Public retrieval exposed portions of each report, section anchors and appended rebuild-plan text; interactive presentation and paid specifications were not verified.

## Preflight: a confident causal argument

[V] Page inspected: https://startups.rip/company/preflight. [E] It uses the shared historical sequence, including traction. Its outcome analysis breaks into revenue versus staffing, platform competition, delayed public launch, capital limits and a late no-code niche. The subsequent plan moves toward plain-language tests for no-code builders, a native runner, repair suggestions and understandable alerts, then exclusions, target metrics and distribution.

[R] Useful: the reader can connect an alleged failure mechanism to a narrower design choice. [E] Thin: the page estimates burn from headcount and infers financing difficulty from absent announcements, while acknowledging that a financing attempt is undocumented. [R] Those are hypotheses, not established causes. Missing public revenue is not evidence that revenue was absent. A targeted integration and a launch target also do not establish technical reliability or customer demand.

## Fig: separate the company from the surviving capability

[V] Page inspected: https://startups.rip/company/fig. [E] It follows the shared sequence. Its outcome subsections examine terminal compatibility, the open specification corpus, follow-through by the buyer and which features survived sunset. Its new proposal is a registry for specification provenance, compatibility, review, migration and release receipts, followed by pilot targets and a commercial hypothesis.

[R] Useful: a report can distinguish independent packaging, retained technology and abandoned adjacent features. That is more informative than a single dead/alive label. [E] Thin: the report leaves important business economics unresolved, and the registry's proposed buyers, willingness to pay and value over repository files remain hypotheses. [R] The move from autocomplete to asset governance is an adjacent opportunity, not a demonstrated reconstruction of the original product.

## Humanloop: the workflow survives while the service ends

[V] Page inspected: https://startups.rip/company/humanloop. [E] The shared sequence covers its product change, evaluation workflow, competitors and traction. Two outcome subsections distinguish continuing expert judgment from customer migration after a team move. The new proposal connects incidents, regression cases, domain review and release evidence; its minimum scope includes blocking critical failures and invalidating stale approvals.

[R] Useful: it makes unknown exit economics explicit and carries evidence into a decision workflow. [E] Thin: proposed pilot pricing and adoption goals are experiments, and the described prototype uses fictional fixtures rather than live releases. [V] A spot-check of the original service's own notice supports the announced September 8, 2025 sunset: https://humanloop.com/docs/changelog/2025/08. [R] That check supports one narrow claim, not every causal conclusion in the report.

## Hamming AI: the report refuses its own failure premise

[V] Page inspected: https://startups.rip/company/hamming-ai. [E] It keeps the shared historical outline but explicitly treats the company as active. Its outcome analysis covers the false failure premise, a narrower voice-testing focus, evaluator reliability and competition. Its new proposal evaluates QA systems through artifact import, a challenge corpus, blind human review, calibration reporting and an evidence packet.

[R] Useful: the report distinguishes vendor-hosted performance claims from external corroboration and asks whether the evaluator itself is calibrated. [E] Thin: the proposed calibration business has unproven prices, corpus targets and buyer demand. [R] An active incumbent is not proof that an adjacent market is empty. Fleet pages should likewise allow “ongoing,” “superseded” and “outcome unknown,” rather than forcing every predecessor into a failure narrative.

## What would make this format worth reading in the Fleet version

[R] Put the decision first: reuse, integrate, investigate or stop. Give the reason, evidence and next uncertainty to resolve. Keep historical facts separate from the author's causal interpretation and from the proposed new work.

[R] Use a dated evidence ledger. Each claim should identify its source, the exact supporting passage or section, what that source can establish and who checked it. A citation to an entire report cannot carry every sentence in a paragraph.

[R] Include competing explanations. An obsolete interface may coexist with a working process. A tool may stop for funding or ownership reasons that new models cannot solve. A success story may still leave a narrow unmet need.

[R] Make the build proposal falsifiable. State the input, expected output, test fixture, allowed actions, disallowed actions, failure behavior and stopping condition. A plausible architecture and a polished mockup do not show that a workflow works.

[R] Show separate statuses for researched, specified, built, run, tested and user validated. Require evidence for each transition. Do not allow generated prose to promote itself into demonstrated capability.
