## Audience, primary task and proposed prototype boundary

Audience: Sailors performing administrative work in an expeditionary or underway unit, with a human reviewer acting in a fictional role. Primary task: capture one synthetic structured training-log form offline, correct validation errors, preserve it locally, reconcile changes from a simulated second replica, explicitly resolve conflicts, review the resulting revision and queue it for simulated routing. This is the smallest proposed complete workflow, an intake recommendation requiring no assumption of an approved Navy form schema. It is a form-and-record workflow, not a generic chat interface.

Observable success: after an acknowledged save, a synthetic draft can be reopened offline with its fields intact; invalid data cannot pass review; competing edits/deletes remain visible; retries create no duplicate effect; explicit resolution produces matching peer states; the current reviewed revision can be queued and its action history inspected. A local queue or simulated receipt must be labeled as such and must never imply delivery to a real recipient. These are future demonstration targets, not evidence of implementation. Full source coverage and operational readiness remain unverified.

Operating environment: a locally served browser prototype, intended for tablet and desktop/narrow layouts with intermittent or absent connectivity after initial provisioning. Offline first-load/provisioning, browser storage availability, restart durability and government device policies are unresolved application integration concerns. No actual cloud or government synchronization endpoint is contacted. Reconciliation and transfer budgeting require deterministic rules; no model should decide conflict winners or reviewer authority.

## Inputs, outputs and minimum data contract

Inputs are synthetic only. Minimum input is a fictional training-log record with a stable record ID, a source-fixture reference, training date, activity, duration and fictional unit/actor labels. Exact fields, formats and domain validation rules are provisional, not official NAVPERS requirements. A synthetic peer revision and deletion case support reconciliation. Any future scan, tasking email, document or attachment must be purpose-made synthetic input; public blank forms are missing references, not supplied integration data.

Revision information must distinguish operation ID, record ID, parent revision(s), originating fictional replica/actor, edited fields and delete intent. Review information must identify the exact revision, decision and fictional reviewer. The record must distinguish draft, validation failure, unresolved conflict, reviewed and queued states. Event order must remain inspectable; timestamps alone must not decide concurrent-edit precedence. Exact serialization and schema belong to planning.

Outputs: the saved synthetic form and visible save status; actionable validation findings; retained competing revisions and an explicit resolution; review decision tied to the current revision; clearly labeled simulated routing queue/receipt; source reference and inspectable action history. Any later timing or error metrics must state sample size, workload, units, baseline availability and synthetic provenance. No extraction, readiness or productivity score is fabricated.

## Human authority and recovery requirements

A person corrects fields, decides conflicts (including edit versus delete), and approves the current resolved revision before routing. Reconciliation is not approval. The prototype must not silently resolve branches or submit on reconnect. Actual reviewer authorization, separation of duties, routing chain and delegation rules require an owner decision; fictional role selection cannot establish authenticated authority.

Recovery targets: preserve drafts after validation errors; distinguish unsaved from durably saved work; retain user input and provide retry on storage failure; retry reconciliation without duplication; keep conflicts visible until reviewed; prevent stale approval after changes; preserve pending routing across interruption where storage permits. Destructive reset/delete must not silently erase unresolved work. Device-loss recovery, retention and backup policy remain unknown.
