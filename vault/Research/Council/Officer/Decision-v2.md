## 2. What to demo, in what order

[R] **Both, with one generated app first and the factory second. No second app on the critical path.** The proposed user is an officer preparing a training draft for review, not someone operating a coding system during a watch. [A] The exact billet, local process and availability of suitable synthetic inputs remain unconfirmed.

[R] Call the app **Training draft**. Bound it to six invented administrative instruction requests, at most four permitted start times per request, two fictional rooms and generic participant IDs. Inputs specify assigned people, invented eligibility labels, available windows, rest gaps and a horizon-wide time budget. Allow a clear table and a changed-input check; omit drag-and-drop and optimization. Every request must appear once in a feasible result. Unknown, invalid or exhausted-search results must remain distinct from proven impossibility within the supplied choices.

[E, `harness/tools/scheduling.ts`] The existing engine checks explicit candidate starts, fixed assigned people, coverage of supplied qualification labels, windows, shared resources, rest intervals and horizon budgets. It returns feasible, infeasible or budget-exhausted status. It does not choose a new roster, infer recurrence, implement SFTRM or provide a finished review/save/export app. Its successful result contains no explanations, so the app must produce a checked comparison with original requests rather than invent a reason for a move.

[R] Exclude autonomous departmental negotiation, authoritative training policy, real rosters, readiness certification, official calendar writes and reconnect sync. Use generic administrative instruction examples throughout. Label coverage **Partial ID 11: synthetic training draft** in the submission and evidence.
