# Verification of the council decisions

## Bottom line

[E] The captured requirements support both proposed problems, but neither proposal establishes user benefit, completed offline behavior or fielding readiness. [P] Synchronization has broader textual support than a requirement confined to cases 36 and 37: the common checklist appears in six cases, the category shared by cases 11 and 24 calls for reconnect sync, and Open Innovation invokes that checklist. [E] Official indexed extracts strongly support a GenAI.mil enterprise designation, but direct official-page and PDF retrieval failed, so that claim remains NOT VERIFIED under this review's access rule. [R] For the smallest independently checkable scheduling experiment, favor Builder's bounded approach; for closer coverage of a case's explicit functional requirements, favor Officer's ID 24 scope, subject to proving extraction and coordinating-role accuracy. [E] The evidence does not select a winner on actual burden removed.

## Scope and verdict rules

[E] Checked November 4, 2026. Officer C01 to C10, Builder 1 to 10 and Judge 1 to 10 refer to each decision's Claims relied on section. The ledger below maps every one of those entries, splitting composite claims where their support differs, and includes material claims elsewhere in the decisions. Proposed screens, staffing, tests, savings and scores are assessed as proposals, not misrepresented as claimed completed work.

[E] The team's later implementation is outside this review. For the earlier project description, the primary artifact is `README.md` at commit `575a3075c9b4d1de914e38797643889d22a05088`; its tracked tree contains only that file. ## What the use cases actually require

[P, S01] **ID 11** is a multi-agent scheduling request, not merely a double-booking detector. Shared operational windows, qualification rosters, fatigue constraints, alternative allocations and mandated training periodicities are explicit. Builder omits departmental negotiation and authentic periodicities. Judge adds invented recurrence/rest constraints and departmental proposal functions, but that still does not implement the requested autonomous negotiation or validate actual policy. [R] Both should retain their partial-case label.

[P, S01] **ID 24** requires extraction for specified commands and identification of the proper coordinating POC. Draft responses and calendar checks are optional. It does not mandate an LLM, particular platform or supplied dataset in its own requirement fields. [R] Officer's deterministic approach is compatible with that wording, but a supported-phrase parser plus invented routing table must be described as a synthetic subset until unseen inputs and role assignment are checked. Its assigned category also carries the sync expectation.

[P, S01] **ID 36** directly targets offline forms, tasking and records workflows with clean reconnect sync, human review and an audit trail. The two scenarios offer distinct emphases. **ID 37** targets the fielding path for an existing tool, with device, approval, security, transport and sustainment evidence; reproducing a laptop calculation would not deliver that path. [E] The case-37 story confirms that the event reports a transition barrier, not that this review independently validated the existing app.

[P, S01] **The sync wording extends beyond 36/37.** The same completion definition appears in IDs 32 through 37, spanning five categories. IDs 11 and 24 both carry the administrative-workflow category description that calls for offline workflows with human review and sync after reconnection. The sixth category, Open Innovation, explicitly requires the shared completion definition. [E] The main rule text does not repeat this complete definition, and the live portal/form could not be read. [R] The defensible reading is a broad published sync expectation with unresolved enforcement for a partial downselect prototype. Treating it as confined to 36/37 would contradict the capture. No seat has evidence for an exemption; none should market its no-sync scope as fully Fleet-ready.

## GenAI.mil designation and its limits

[E, S17] Searches returned substantial official DON CIO extracts for the January 28 designation memo, its PDF, a February 24 explanatory article and a March 16 follow-up. Direct retrieval of the article, memo, PDF, mobile versions, indexed alternate paths and official publication copy returned rejection or fetch errors. Under the requested standard, the designation and detailed policy scope stay NOT VERIFIED despite strong indexed corroboration.

[V, indexed only, S17] The extracts describe a mandatory enterprise generative-AI service for authorized CUI/IL5 use, a transition deadline, capability reporting and consolidation. [A] If the full memo matches those extracts, its practical meaning is an enterprise governance and adoption requirement. It would not, by itself, demonstrate that a custom application is approved, an integration/API exists, the service works disconnected, or event data restrictions are waived. [R] Carry this as a policy-verification gap before making any enterprise-alternative pitch. It does not by itself invalidate a deterministic synthetic local application.

## Numbers, burden and disconnected operation

[E] No decision supplies an observed task-specific saving, representative throughput, measured error reduction or validated annual impact number. The hypothetical scoring totals are arithmetically correct, but their score inputs and uncertainty ranges are not evidence. The 60/90-second interactions are usability targets. Builder's 20-hour budget and 4,096-combination bound are arithmetic, not observed delivery or runtime performance. Six-event fixtures, two/three reviewers and a 20-message test set define an experiment; they do not establish Fleet representativeness.

[V, S05] The fatigue evidence concerns the report's 2020 officer survey and recent deployment experience. It supports minimizing attention demands, with no demonstrated causal link to time saved by either candidate. [V, S16] The maintenance report concerns personnel, training and logistics-system problems. [R] Neither report should be converted into hours reclaimed by a message parser or scheduling app.

[E/V, S06 to S09] Local database/server primitives and a local scheduling implementation route are supported. [A] All three complete offline application promises remain untested in these decisions: cold start, new input, useful computation, correction, acknowledged save, restart, export and absence of external runtime dependencies must be demonstrated on a named artifact. [R] Keep separate claims for local use after setup, installing on a disconnected clean machine, local model inference/software generation, reconnect sync and authorized afloat use. Evidence for one does not establish the others. Local save and CSV export do not satisfy reconnect synchronization.
