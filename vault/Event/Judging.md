# Judging

Source: portal Judging Criteria page, restated in our own words [P].

| Criterion | Weight | What judges ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warfighting and deckplate impact | 30% | How much watchstander cognitive burden does it remove, or how many deckplate hours does it give back? |
| Speed to Fleet | 20% | How fast could it be fielded at the edge in disconnected afloat (DDIL) conditions? |
| Fleet scalability | 20% | How far does it spread across ship classes, ratings or operational domains? |
| Barrier removal | 15% | How well does it remove systemic bottlenecks, manual data entry and legacy friction? |
| Judges' discretion | 15% | How strong are the technical execution, Sailor user experience and innovation? |

- [P] Each criterion is required and scored from 0 to 100 points. The page gives a 100-point maximum.
- [A] The final score is the weighted sum of the five scores. The page does not spell out the formula.
- [P] Scores stay hidden until all judging is finished.

## What this implies

- [R] Half the score (impact plus speed) rewards a narrow, measurable burden reduction that runs offline. Lead the demo with a number: hours saved or steps removed.
- [R] Decide early what runs with no connection, because Speed to Fleet is judged under DDIL conditions and a cloud-only demo will struggle there.
- [R] A hosted model API is a fine way to build fast. Be ready to say what the disconnected version looks like.
