# Rules

Restated in our own words from the portal's Rules tab [P]. The portal is authoritative.

## Purpose

The Fleet needs capabilities that give Sailors time back, ease watchstander burden, improve decisions and hold up when contested or disconnected. Build for the person on watch at 2 AM rather than for the judge. Every team should leave with evidence, lessons and a believable next step.

## Three challenge categories

1. **Naval Agentic AI.** Trustworthy, observable and governable agents for Navy missions. A solution must define what the agent may do, what it must never do, what needs human approval, how activity is logged and how the agent is stopped.
2. **Bring Your Own Idea.** Any operational, administrative, maintenance, logistics, personnel or readiness problem, from any service or government organization, as long as it clearly matters to warfighter missions.
3. **Digital Challenge.** Demonstrate, compare or improve tools that Sailors and warfighters already built. Teams may merge competing solutions, package a capability for wider use or prove which approach best fits Fleet needs.

The portal's use cases use six different categories. See [[Reference/Open-Questions]].

## Twelve core rules

| # | Rule | What it requires of us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Align to an ROI | Tie the work to an ROI below |
| 2 | Solve a real problem | Name the user, the problem, the current process and the measurable burden reduced |
| 3 | Build, test, demonstrate | Slides alone do not count. Produce a prototype, test, workflow, integration or technical finding people can observe |
| 4 | Stay unclassified | No classified, CUI, operationally sensitive, PII, export-controlled or nonpublic government data unless vetted or approved |
| 5 | Use authorized resources | Approved commercial, government, open-source or personally provided tools are fine, but disclose tooling, models, datasets and external services. See [[Sprint/Tool-Disclosure-Log]] |
| 6 | Preserve human authority | Show human approval points, prohibited actions, monitoring, escalation and shutdown |
| 7 | Be open about prior work | Existing work is welcome, but separate it from what was done at the event. See [[Sprint/Prior-Work-Log]] |
| 8 | No sales pitches | Industry contributes capability or expertise. Judging looks at mission results |
| 9 | Design for Fleet conditions | Consider cybersecurity, portability, limited connectivity, shipboard constraints, sustainment and disconnected operation |
| 10 | Document the friction | Record policy, data, authority, infrastructure, acquisition and technical barriers. See [[Sprint/Friction-Log]] |
| 11 | Plan beyond Friday | Name a mission owner, technical owner, next experiment and required resources, and give a 30/60/90-day transition recommendation |
| 12 | Act professionally | No harassment, unauthorized access, destructive testing or malicious activity |

## OSW ROI mapping

| ROI | What counts as evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Developing the skills of a digital workforce | Skills practiced, tools learned, cross-functional work, confidence gained |
| Accelerating platform adoption | Solutions packaged or shown on enterprise offerings or another reusable path toward deployment and authorization |
| Clarifying friction points to agile delivery | Documented barriers, the responsible organizations, recommended fixes |
| Promoting a culture of innovation | New ideas surfaced, Sailor-builders connected, mission staff empowered |
| Learning and adaptation during war | Evidence of better operational learning, decision speed, resilience or adaptation under contested conditions |

## What winning means

A winner shows more than good software. It shows a real Fleet problem can be attacked differently, brings evidence of what works, develops its people and sets out a believable route from prototype to operational capability.
